# Decision Tree

A **Decision Tree** is a supervised machine learning algorithm used for **classification and regression**.

It makes decisions by repeatedly splitting the data based on different features, forming a tree-like structure.

A Decision Tree consists of:

- **Root Node** → The starting point of the tree.
- **Branches** → Connections representing the outcome of a decision.
- **Decision/Internal Nodes** → Nodes where a feature is used to make a decision.
- **Leaf Nodes** → Final output or prediction.

### Example of a Decision Tree

Suppose we want to decide what type of food to eat based on the amount of **money available**.

```python
money = ?

if money < 30:
    Snack

elif money > 30 and money < 60:
    Fast Food

elif money > 60 and money < 90:
    Dinner

else:
    Fine Dining
```

## How to Choose the Root Node?

The **root node** is the feature with the **highest Information Gain**.

1. Calculate the Entropy of the dataset.
2. Calculate the Information Gain for each feature.
3. Select the feature with the highest Information Gain.
4. Make branches and repeat the process for all the branches.
5. Stop when :
   a. All data is pure
   b. Maximum depth is reached
---

### Entropy

**Entropy** measures the **impurity or uncertainty** in a dataset.

$$
H(S) = -\sum_{i=1}^{c} p_i \log_2(p_i)
$$

For binary classification:

$$
H(S) =
-p_{Yes}\log_2(p_{Yes})
-p_{No}\log_2(p_{No})
$$

- **Entropy = 0** → Pure
- **Entropy = 1** → Maximum impurity
- Higher entropy → More uncertainty

---

### Information Gain

**Information Gain** measures the reduction in uncertainty after a split.

$$
IG(S,A) =
H(S) -
\sum_v
\frac{|S_v|}{|S|}
H(S_v)
$$

Where:

- $S$ → Original dataset
- $A$ → Feature used for splitting
- $S_v$ → Subset after splitting

$$
IG = \text{Entropy Before Split} - \text{Weighted Entropy After Split}
$$

**Highest Information Gain → Best Split**

---

### Decision Tree Process

$$
\text{Entropy}
\rightarrow
\text{Information Gain}
\rightarrow
\text{Best Feature}
\rightarrow
\text{Split}
$$


### Step 1: Calculate Entropy of the Complete Dataset

From the dataset:

- **Yes = 9**
- **No = 5**
- **Total = 14**

$$
H(S) =
-\frac{9}{14}\log_2\left(\frac{9}{14}\right)
-\frac{5}{14}\log_2\left(\frac{5}{14}\right)
$$

$$
H(S) \approx 0.940
$$

---

### Step 2: Calculate Entropy of Overcast

For **Outlook = Overcast**:

- **Yes = 4**
- **No = 0**
- **Total = 4**

$$
H(Overcast) =
-\frac{4}{4}\log_2\left(\frac{4}{4}\right)
-\frac{0}{4}\log_2\left(\frac{0}{4}\right)
$$

$$
H(Overcast) = 0
$$

The Overcast subset is **pure** because all observations are **Yes**.

---

### Step 3: Calculate Information Gain

Information Gain is:

$$
IG(S,A) = H(S) - \sum_v\frac{|S_v|}{|S|}H(S_v)
$$

For **Outlook**, the subsets are:

- Sunny → 5 samples
- Overcast → 4 samples
- Rain → 5 samples

Their entropies are:

$$
H(Sunny) \approx 0.971
$$

$$
H(Overcast) = 0
$$

$$
H(Rain) \approx 0.971
$$

Therefore:

$$
IG(S,Outlook) = 0.940 - \left(\frac{5}{14}(0.971)+\frac{4}{14}(0)+\frac{5}{14}(0.971)\right)
$$

$$
IG(S,Outlook) = 0.940 - 0.694
$$

$$
\boxed{IG(S,Outlook) \approx 0.246}
$$

### Result

- **Entropy of Complete Dataset = 0.940**
- **Entropy of Overcast = 0**
- **Information Gain of Outlook = 0.246**

**Therefore, Outlook has the highest Information Gain and becomes the Root Node.**
